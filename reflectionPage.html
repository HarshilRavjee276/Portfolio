Professional Reflection

What I've learned during this module

Building this portfolio for ITPP5112 pushed me to think about my technical work the way an employer would see it, rather than the way an assessor grades it. It's one thing to submit a Java program for a mark; it's another to explain, in plain language, why that project matters and what it says about how I work. I had to plan a whole website's structure and navigation before writing a line of code, apply basic SEO so the site can actually be found, and make sure everything held up on both desktop and mobile. That combination of planning, writing, and technical execution is a good preview of what real project work looks like.

My strongest IT professional skill right now

My strongest skill at the moment is communication — specifically, translating technical detail into something a non-technical audience can follow. That's come from a mix of places: serving as Class Representative, where I had to relay student concerns to lecturers clearly and constructively, and Toastmasters, where I advanced to a school competition as top evaluator. Paired with a genuinely collaborative approach to teamwork, it means I can sit at the table between "the people who build the system" and "the people who use it."

The skill I'd like to improve

I want to strengthen my systems architecture and design thinking — moving beyond writing working code to planning how an application should be structured before I start, so it stays maintainable as it grows. Right now most of my hands-on experience has been on smaller, self-contained programs like the ones in my Projects section; I want to get more comfortable designing larger systems with multiple moving parts.

How I plan to develop it

My Bachelor's degree is specifically aimed at application development and systems architecture, so I'll be building that foundation formally through coursework. Alongside that, I intend to take on larger personal projects that go beyond a single class or script, and contribute to open-source repositories to see how experienced developers structure real codebases and how real IT systems are designed to fit business needs, not just technical requirements.

How this portfolio supports my future studies and career

This portfolio gives me a living record I can keep building on rather than starting from scratch each time I apply for something. As I complete more of my degree, add projects, and pick up certifications, I can update this same site instead of rebuilding my professional story from zero. For internships and entry-level roles, it also gives potential employers something my CV can't: a working example of code I've written, a site I've published, and proof that I can take a project from a written requirement through to something live and publicly accessible — which is, in the end, most of what the job actually involves.
